# OrgAnchor Showcase Policy

Status: Active policy for official examples, case studies, and public listings.

## Purpose

The OrgAnchor showcase is not a registry of everyone who uses the tool.

It is a curated public surface for organizations that demonstrate the kind of long-term, evidence-bearing, accountable adoption OrgAnchor wants to encourage.

## Showcase Is Not Certification

Being listed by OrgAnchor does not mean:

```text
OrgAnchor certifies the organization.
OrgAnchor guarantees its claims are true.
OrgAnchor endorses its products or services.
OrgAnchor has audited every artifact.
```

A listing means only that the official project has chosen to highlight a use case that appears aligned with OrgAnchor's purpose and adoption principles at the time of review.

## Minimum Listing Requirements

An organization should generally have:

- A public `/verify` page.
- A signed official endpoint statement.
- A root authority record.
- Machine-readable `organchor.json`.
- Public claims and evidence manifests when product or service claims are central to the listing.
- Plain-language limits on what the evidence proves.
- No known unresolved impersonation or severe deception concern.

## Preferred Qualities

Stronger showcase candidates:

- Publish meaningful evidence, not only a signed endpoint.
- Separate claims from evidence.
- Identify first-party and third-party evidence.
- Record superseded claims and corrections.
- Keep a visible migration history when roots or endpoints change.
- Preserve successive signed snapshots and distinguish declared dates from
  independently anchored publication times.
- Make verification understandable to non-experts.
- Make verification usable by AI agents.

## Reasons To Decline Or Remove

OrgAnchor may decline or remove a listing if there is credible reason to believe the organization is using OrgAnchor to:

- Impersonate another organization.
- Hide a material identity break.
- Present signed continuity as proof of moral trustworthiness.
- Launder fraud, scams, exploitation, or deliberate deception.
- Misrepresent evidence or remove important context.
- Claim OrgAnchor endorsement without permission.
- Attack or harass people under the appearance of official verification.

Removal from the showcase is not a protocol judgment. It is an editorial and project-governance decision.

## Review Inputs

Showcase review may consider:

- Public OrgAnchor artifacts.
- The organization's own claims and evidence manifests.
- Public corrections or disputes.
- Whether the organization accurately describes what OrgAnchor proves.
- Whether the listing would help users understand the tool honestly.

The review should avoid becoming a broad political, legal, or moral tribunal. The key question is whether the use case aligns with OrgAnchor's stated purpose and whether showcasing it would mislead users.

## No Paid Guarantee

Payment, sponsorship, partnership, or contribution should not guarantee showcase placement.

If the project later accepts sponsorships, sponsored material must remain separate from showcase judgment.

## Appeals and Corrections

An organization may ask for a listing to be reconsidered if:

- It fixes misleading OrgAnchor language.
- It publishes missing verification artifacts.
- It corrects or supersedes problematic claims.
- It provides additional context for disputed evidence.

Changes should be handled by publishing new signed artifacts when possible, not by silently rewriting history.

## Good Showcase Language

Use:

```text
This organization publishes a verifiable OrgAnchor identity continuity trail.
Its official endpoints, root authority, claims, evidence, and carrier receipts can be independently inspected.
Where multiple signed and time-anchored snapshots exist, reviewers can inspect
how those records changed over time.
```

Avoid:

```text
This organization is verified as trustworthy.
This product is proven by OrgAnchor.
This organization is officially certified by OrgAnchor.
```

The showcase should make the world clearer, not more easily fooled.
